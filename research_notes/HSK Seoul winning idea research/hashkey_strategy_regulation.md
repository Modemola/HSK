## 2. Hong Kong regulatory environment

### Takeaway
The HK Stablecoins Ordinance (Cap. 656) has been in force since 1 Aug 2025, and licensing is very selective: 2 of 36 applicants by mid-2026 (HSBC and Anchorpoint). Only licensed issuers' stablecoins may be offered to retail. A new SFC dealer/custodian licensing bill was gazetted on 20 Nov 2026. The Travel Rule is strict, with full originator data at ≥HK$8,000. For a hackathon, the stablecoin you use matters: HKDAP or regulated USD stablecoins are on-narrative, and USDT for retail payments is not.

### Cited Findings
- HKMA granted the first two stablecoin issuer licences to **HSBC** and **Anchorpoint Financial** (StanChart/HKT/Animoca JV). There were 36 applications by the 30 Sep 2025 deadline, roughly a 5.6% approval rate. Focus areas were reserves, risk management and AML. Dates conflict: CoinDesk's URL is dated 24 Mar 2026, and other sources say 10 Apr 2026. — [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/03/24/hong-kong-awards-first-stablecoin-licenses-to-hsbc-standard-chartered-led-group); [Elliptic](https://www.elliptic.co/insights/hong-kong-awards-first-stablecoin-licenses-to-hsbc-and-standard-chartered/); [Stablecoin Insider](https://stablecoininsider.org/hkma-stablecoin-issuer-licenses/)
- As of May 2026 there were still only 2 licences and no timetable for more rounds. HSBC is targeting an HKD stablecoin in H2 2026, and Anchorpoint planned phased issuance from Q2 2026 (HKDAP is now in beta). — [fintechlaw.ai](https://fintechlaw.ai/blog/hong-kong-stablecoin-regulation-two-licenses-36-applicants); [Cosmoverse](https://www.cosmoverse.org/insights/hong-kong-has-licensed-stablecoins-what-institutions-need-to-decide-now)
- Only fiat-referenced stablecoins (FRS) from HKMA-licensed issuers may be offered to retail in HK. CDD is required for issuance/redemption ≥HK$8,000. — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/hong-kongs-stablecoin-regulations-unveiled-bill-passed-licensee-guidelines/); [Mayer Brown (2025)](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/hong-kongs-stablecoin-bill-key-amendments-and-next-steps-following-legislative-passage)
- Under the SFC/AMLO **Travel Rule**, rules apply to VA transfers regardless of amount, with fuller originator/beneficiary data required at **≥HK$8,000**. For transfers to unhosted or custodial wallets, licensees must identify the wallet provider or the ownership. — [Notabene](https://notabene.id/world/hong-kong)
- A bill was gazetted on **20 Nov 2026** amending AMLO to license VA **dealing, advisory, management and custody** under the SFC. First LegCo reading is 2 Dec 2026, and dealers must hold client assets with SFC-licensed custodians. — [CoinReporter](https://www.coinreporter.io/2026/11/hong-kong-crypto-bill-gazetted-dealers-advisers-managers-custodians/); [Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/hong-kong-publishes-bill-to-license-crypto-dealers-advisers-and-custodians/)
- Beijing has reportedly moved to halt some large tech-backed stablecoin projects in HK. — [cited in search summary of TradingView/Cointelegraph](https://www.tradingview.com/news/cointelegraph:89c675b35094b:0-how-hashkey-plans-to-become-hong-kong-s-first-crypto-ipo/) (single source, unverified)

### Inferences
- An AI agent that "manages money for others" or executes trades on behalf of users may come close to the new SFC **VA management/advisory** licensing categories. Pitch trading agents as **user-owned, non-custodial, user-set limits** (a session-key or vault the user controls) to avoid "unlicensed asset manager" objections.
- Agent-to-agent payment flows above HK$8,000 trigger Travel Rule data duties. An idea that **natively attaches Travel-Rule/KYC credentials (e.g., ZKID or SBT attestations) to HSP mandates** solves a real compliance pain point and matches the White Paper 2.0 agent identity layer.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether more HK stablecoin licences were granted in Jun–Nov 2026, or whether HSBC's HKD coin has launched. Check the HKMA register.
- No HK-specific guidance found on AI agents executing VA transactions.

## 3. South Korea regulatory environment

### Takeaway
As of 25 Nov 2026, **KRW stablecoins are not yet legal to issue**. The FSC unveiled the government Digital Asset Basic Act draft on 24 Nov 2026: bank-majority (>50%) consortia issue KRW stablecoins ("digital payment tokens"), and exchange shareholders are capped at 20%. A hearing is reportedly set for 3 Dec, and the bill still needs National Assembly passage. Separately, the **FX Transactions Act amendment** (passed 7 May 2026) brings cross-border VA transfers under FX law with a registration duty, reportedly effective **3 Dec 2026**, the same day as the hackathon deadline. Hwanchigi (crypto-based illegal FX) is a top enforcement priority, and USDT is the main tool. Foreign-tourist stablecoin payments (USDC→KRW merchant settlement) are the one use case regulators are actively piloting.

### Cited Findings
**Digital Asset Basic Act / KRW stablecoins**
- The FSC presented the government draft on 24 Nov 2026. KRW stablecoin issuers would initially be **consortia where banks collectively hold >50%**. A fintech or tech firm can be the single largest shareholder. FSC approval and full backing by deposits or trusts are required. Stablecoins are legally defined as "digital payment tokens". — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/digital-assets/2026/11/16/south-korea-will-let-tech-platforms-lead-won-stablecoins-from-behind-a-bank-majority-while-taiwan-wants-one-bank-in-charge/); [Digital Today](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/130832/digital-asset-basic-law-bill-unveiled-with-stablecoins-excludes-corporates-and-etfs); [CoinReporter](https://www.coinreporter.io/2026/11/south-korea-digital-asset-basic-act-20-percent-exchange-ownership-cap/)
- Exchange major shareholders are capped at 20% (34% with FSC approval). The draft reportedly lifts the 9-year domestic ICO ban. It excludes corporate market access, ETFs and derivatives. — [Digital Today](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/130228/digital-asset-basic-law-stake-ownership-cap-corporate-market-access-left-out); [shattered.io](https://shattered.io/south-korea-digital-asset-basic-act-draft-domestic-icos-won-stablecoins-nov-24/)
- A public hearing is reportedly scheduled for 3 Dec 2026 (aggregator source). No KRW stablecoin issuer has been approved. A bank-led group formed in Jan 2026 but has announced no product. — [Newscord](https://newscord.org/article/south-korea-schedules-dec-3-hearing-for-digital-asset-basic-act-on-won-stablecoi--Story_20261125_SouthKoreascryptobil3e9580e7); [OneSafe](https://www.onesafe.io/blog/south-korea-won-stablecoin-bill-bank-majority-exchange-cap)

**Cross-border / FX / hwanchigi**
- The National Assembly passed the **FX Transactions Act amendment on 7 May 2026**. It brings cross-border VA transfers under FX law for the first time and creates a registered "virtual-asset transfer service" category. Reporting goes to the Bank of Korea, and it covers exchange↔private wallet transfers. The effective date is reported as **3 Dec 2026** (unconfirmed officially). — [BKL Legal Update](https://www.bkl.co.kr/en/law/insight/newsletter/6819); [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/south-korea-crypto-transfer-rules-december-2026/); [Coingape](https://coingape.com/south-korea-to-impose-fx-controls-on-crypto-transfers-from-december/)
- Oct 2026: Korea plans to classify stablecoins used in cross-border payments as "means of payment" under FX law. The integrated draft reportedly **exempts certain stablecoin payments for goods and services from FX reporting within a defined scope**. Cointelegraph could not verify this against a public filing. — [The Block](https://theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-10-23-south-korea-stablecoins-payment-instruments-424028); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/south-korea-stablecoin-fx-law-rwa-trust-draft)
- Hwanchigi: Korea Customs referred US$7.1bn of crypto-linked crime (2021–Aug 2025), about 90% (US$6.4bn) of it hwanchigi. USDT is the preferred tool. Jan 2026: a US$113m ring was sent to prosecutors under the FX Act. May 2026: ₩600bn in illegal FX deals were uncovered. — [Crystal Intelligence](https://crystalintelligence.com/investigations/inside-south-koreas-7-1b-crypto-crime-problem/); [Cryptopolitan](https://www.cryptopolitan.com/south-korea-customs-illegal-crypto-ring/); [Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/05/04/illegal-forex-deals-worth-600-billion-won-uncovered-in-korea)
- Oct 2026: a proposed FX enforcement-decree change would expel currency-exchange businesses on the **first detected "coin hawala" case** ("one strike out"). Only about 10% of 2026 hwanchigi fines had been collected. — [Asia Business Daily](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026100708515075717); [Asia Business Daily](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026070610040847023)

**Tourism / payments pilots (the green-lit use case)**
- **BC Card + Coinbase + Wavebridge** (Aug 2026) completed a pilot in which foreign visitors paid **USDC from wallets at BC QR merchants, with KRW settlement to merchants**. An earlier BC Card prepaid stablecoin card (Dec 2025) is limited to foreign tourists under current rules. — [icobench](https://icobench.com/news/bc-card-completes-stablecoin-payment-pilot-for-foreign-tourists-in-south-korea/); [BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/009e36d0-eaab-428c-8eeb-218cb9fbb779)

### Inferences
- The live regulatory risk in Korea is anything that looks like **KRW↔crypto cross-border value transfer outside registered channels** (hwanchigi). An agent that autonomously routes funds between Korean and foreign wallets, or arbitrages a "kimchi premium", would draw immediate objections from a Korean audience, especially with the FX amendment taking effect on 3 Dec.
- **Issuing a KRW stablecoin** (even a mock) is not yet legal and will be bank-controlled. Use a "KRW-denominated invoice settled in a regulated USD/HKD stablecoin" framing, or "KRW-stablecoin-ready, plug in a bank-consortium issuer later".
- The tourist-payment pattern (foreign stablecoin in, KRW to merchant via a licensed acquirer) has regulatory cover through multiple bank/card pilots and the proposed goods-and-services FX exemption. It maps directly onto HSP's offline retail push (Pandabot) and the HashKey–Kbank corridor.

### Gaps
- Official FSC/BoK confirmation of the 3 Dec 2026 FX amendment effective date was not found.
- No Korea-specific rules found on AI agents conducting crypto trades or payments.
- No public detail found on pentabase's own products or Korean partners beyond organising the hackathon.

## 4. Implications for a hackathon idea

### Takeaway
The safest idea that fits HashKey's goals combines **HSP agent payments with compliance built in, aimed at a real HK↔KR commerce corridor**. Examples: an agent that settles merchant or tourist purchases or B2B invoices through HSP mandates, using a licensed or regulated stablecoin (HKDAP/RDUSD/USDC.e), with on-chain identity/Travel-Rule attestations and spend limits, so every payment is auditable. For Track 1, the trading agent should be non-custodial and user-bounded, with on-chain risk limits and explainable, verifiable decisions. It should not resemble discretionary asset management or cross-border arbitrage.

### Cited Findings (anchors for the pitch)
- HashKey's own framing is that HSP produces "auditable, compliant financial credentials" and solves "agent payment authorization (mandates)". — [EIN Presswire](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/956165566/hashkey-hsp-expands-offline-retail-footprint-via-on-chain-settlement-integration-with-pandabot-caf)
- Citi's agentic-payments revenue thesis (HK$1.59bn by 2030) and AP2 alliance membership. — [HashKey/Citi](https://group.hashkey.com/en/citi-initiates-coverage-on-hashkey-with-buy-rating-hk5-6-target-price/)
- White Paper 2.0 agent stack: identity (ZKID), credit, assets. — [PANews](https://panews.io/articles/019db90c-9876-7411-88e4-130cbb3951a5)
- HashKey–Kbank KR↔HK remittance/settlement MOU with KYC/AML. — [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/hashkey-taps-kbank-bpmg-in-south-korea-stablecoin-payments-push/)
- Korea tourist USDC→KRW merchant pilots (BC Card/Coinbase, Hana/Circle, SBI/NICE with an AI-agent check). — [icobench](https://icobench.com/news/bc-card-completes-stablecoin-payment-pilot-for-foreign-tourists-in-south-korea/); [CoinGabbar](https://www.coingabbar.com/en/japan-korea-stablecoin-ai-agent-checks-tourist-crypto-payments)
- HashKey Capital/AMBR stress human oversight and limits on agent autonomy. — [Fintech News HK](https://fintechnews.hk/41964/hong-kong-fintech-week-news/hashkey-capital-ambr-partnership-ai-agents/)

### Inferences
**Do (aligned with HashKey and regulators):**
1. Use **HSP Cart/Payment Mandates (AP2 shape) + x402**, since HSP is the product HashKey is commercialising. Show a mandate with spend caps, expiry, payee allowlist and revocation.
2. Attach **compliance as a feature**: agent identity (SBT/ZK credential), KYC-tier attestation of the principal, a Travel-Rule data payload for transfers ≥HK$8,000 (or an equivalent threshold), a sanctions/risk check before settlement, and an on-chain audit receipt. This is the "Compliance-as-a-Service" story even though I could not confirm CaaS as an official product name.
3. Pick a **Korea-flavoured, regulator-blessed use case**: foreign tourist/agent pays a Korean merchant in a regulated stablecoin and the merchant receives KRW via a licensed acquirer; or HK↔KR SME invoice settlement mirroring the Kbank MOU. State plainly that "KRW leg via licensed partner; no P2P KRW conversion".
4. Settle in **HKDAP / RDUSD / USDC.e**, avoiding USDT for payment flows, since USDT is the hwanchigi tool of choice in Korean enforcement coverage. Note that the HSKSwap pool cited by participants is WHSK/USDT, which is fine for Track 1 trading but should be avoided as the payment rail in Track 2.
5. Track 1: give the trading agent **non-custodial session keys and an on-chain risk vault** (loss caps, slippage caps, whitelisted pools), plus verifiable/explainable trade logs. Possibly let it pay for data/signals via HSP x402, which links both tracks.

**Avoid (likely regulatory objection from a KR/HK panel):**
- Agents that move value KRW↔foreign crypto peer-to-peer, kimchi-premium arbitrage bots, or "remittance without a licensed partner". These read as hwanchigi, and the FX amendment takes effect around the hackathon deadline (3 Dec).
- Minting a "KRW stablecoin" yourself. It is not legal yet and will be bank-majority under the draft.
- Retail-facing agents that pool and manage third-party funds. This is close to the new HK SFC VA management/advisory licensing (bill gazetted 20 Nov 2026).

**Differentiation note:** Many public Track 2 repos already ship "x402 + HSP mandate + receipt registry/escrow" (e.g., [astra-tc](https://github.com/astra-tc/hsp-a2a-settlement), [NeelShah1505](https://github.com/NeelShah1505/hsk-a2a-settlement), [payminto-clearing](https://github.com/sagarjethi/payminto-clearing), [risklayer](https://github.com/SatyamDev803/risklayer)). Many Track 1 repos already ship "AI trader + risk vault" ([Keel](https://github.com/yayguru/keel-hsk), [SayTrade](https://github.com/Pras454/SayTrade), [HorizonQuant](https://github.com/alphonshhhomenicmtu/horizonquant-hsk)). The open space is the **real-world Korea commerce/corridor plus compliance-credential layer**, rather than another generic A2A escrow.
